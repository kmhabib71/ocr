What is the Finance Act (অর্থ আইন)?
The Finance Act is a yearly law passed by the Parliament (usually in June) to implement the government’s financial proposals for that fiscal year.

Its main jobs:

Change or amend existing laws (Income Tax Act, VAT Act, Customs Act, etc.).

Introduce new tax rates, duties, or exemptions.

Enforce temporary or annual tax provisions (like minimum tax rates, surcharges, tax holidays).

Each Finance Act is numbered separately (e.g., Finance Act 2024 is “২০২৪ সনের ০৫ নং আইন”).

What is the Income Tax Act (আয়কর আইন, ২০২৩)?
The Income Tax Act is the permanent law that contains the core rules, definitions, and procedures for income taxation.

It has all chapters, sections, and schedules governing:

Who pays tax

How income is calculated

Filing returns, audits, appeals

Penalties, assessments, exemptions

It doesn’t change every year. Instead, the Finance Act updates it annually (e.g., changes rates, thresholds, or specific provisions).

Key Differences
Purpose

Finance Act = Temporary, yearly update & amendments.

Income Tax Act = Permanent base law.

Structure

Income Tax Act = Organized by Chapter → Section → Sub-section → Clause → Schedules.

Finance Act = Organized by Chapters (Intro + various laws), with serialized sections (১, ২, ৩… all the way through, across chapters) that say:

“Amend ধারা ৩ক of Income Tax Act…”

“Replace Table-1 in VAT Act…”

So Finance Act’s section numbers are sequential, not restarting per chapter.

Cross-linking

Finance Act sections do not stand alone — they always reference specific sections of other Acts.

To understand a Finance Act, you must trace each amendment back to the base Act (Income Tax, VAT, etc.).

How they work together
Think of it like:

Income Tax Act (Core Law)
↑
Finance Act (Yearly patch notes, changing rates/sections)
↑
SROs (Government notifications further clarifying or adding rules)
